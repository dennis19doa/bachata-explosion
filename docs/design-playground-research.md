# Bachata Explosion design playground — research and directions

Updated: 24 August 2026

## What the site must keep

- The existing event structure: Berlin Bachata Festival, Jack & Jill, Role Rotation and Elite Dance.
- The history and complete photography archive instead of treating past events as disposable content.
- The team, ambassadors, volunteers, contact information and practical pages.
- A direct ticket path for visitors arriving from Instagram.
- The dark, energetic, dancer-built spirit of Bachata Explosion.

The goal is therefore not a smaller generic landing page. It is a clearer first visit wrapped around the same full event world.

## Festival research

### Bachata Geneva Festival

Source: [bachatagenevafestival.com](https://bachatagenevafestival.com/)

Strong patterns:

- Date, venue, short promise, countdown and primary ticket action are presented together.
- The venue is treated as part of the product, including airport distance, accommodation and production benefits.
- The festival owns one memorable theme, “Festival of Nations,” and repeats it throughout the experience.
- WhatsApp is used as the between-editions community layer.
- Practical questions live in a prominent FAQ rather than being scattered through promotional copy.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow: information hierarchy, venue confidence, a compact FAQ and a community path.

What it should not borrow: the “nations” identity, the prestige language or its visual style. Berlin Bachata Festival needs its own story.

### Croatian Summer Salsa Festival

Sources: [festival homepage](https://www.crosalsafestival.com/2026), [interactive program](https://www.crosalsafestival.com/program)

Strong patterns:

- It sells a destination and a lifestyle, not only workshops and parties.
- Large proof points make scale understandable: days, countries, dancers and workshops.
- Each signature experience—beach party, boat party, concert—is given its own visual chapter.
- Its schedule can be filtered by type, reducing cognitive load in a very large program.
- Travel, accommodation and venue information are treated as purchase-critical content.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow: signature moments, filterable schedules and the idea that Berlin/MOA are part of the experience.

### Barcelona Dance Congress

Source: [bdcongress.com](https://www.bdcongress.com/)

Strong patterns:

- A short positioning statement leads into four clear product pillars.
- Artist cards are categorized by dance style and role.
- Ticket types are compared in place, including hotel packages.
- The official venue is presented as a central product benefit.
- FAQ, contact, social links and buying information are visible without leaving the main experience.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow: structured artist discovery, clear package comparison and product pillars.

What it should avoid: extremely long artist grids on the homepage. The Explosion homepage should preview the lineup and let the festival page carry the full roster.

### Bachaturo

Source: [bachaturo.com](https://bachaturo.com/)

Strong patterns:

- Scale is communicated immediately through artist, floor, workshop-hour and room counts.
- Program, hotel, transport, tickets and customer account are all first-class destinations.
- Background video and optional sound establish event energy quickly.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow: operational clarity and proof points.

What it should avoid: putting the full timetable and long operational notices on the homepage.

### A’DAM Bachata Festival

Source: [adambachatafestival.com](https://www.adambachatafestival.com/)

Strong pattern: multiple related event brands are presented as a family with a clear “learn more / buy now” pair.

Weak pattern: the contact form does not help users choose the right topic, and public communication still relies on a generic Gmail address. Bachata Explosion can look more trustworthy by keeping its domain email and routing questions visibly.

### Paris Bachata Festival

Source: [parisbachatafestival.com](https://www.parisbachatafestival.com/)

Useful baseline: countdown, ticket action, artists, program, venue and language switching.

Lesson: these elements are now category expectations, not a distinctive design idea. Bachata Explosion needs them but cannot rely on them alone.

### Sónar

Source: [sonar.es](https://sonar.es/en)

Strong patterns:

- Very little text is required to communicate music, innovation and creative culture.
- Motion and changing campaign graphics can refresh the site each edition while the information architecture remains stable.
- The archive is used to keep completed editions alive through recordings and editorial stories.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow: edition-based visual campaigns and “relive” content.

### DGTL

Source: [dgtl-festival.com](https://dgtl-festival.com/en/)

Strong patterns:

- A global brand can contain multiple editions without flattening their identities.
- Raw industrial settings are a core visual motif rather than a generic background.
- Festival, record label, clothing, sustainability experiments and news form one cultural universe.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow: Berlin materiality, a recognizable visual system, and related content between events.

### Tomorrowland

Source: [tomorrowland.com](https://www.tomorrowland.com/)

Strong pattern: each edition is treated as a chapter in a larger fictional world. Aftermovies, stories, products, radio and future events extend the brand beyond ticket sales.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow at a much smaller scale: name each edition, give it a campaign motif, and let past chapters remain explorable.

### Unsound

Source: [unsound.pl](https://www.unsound.pl/en/unsound-2026)

Strong patterns:

- Experimental visual identity does not prevent a very direct navigation: artists, tickets, archive, schedule and FAQ.
- The archive is a first-class navigation destination covering many years.
- Editorial news explains the thinking behind the program.

What Bachata Explosion should borrow: permission to be visually strange while remaining structurally obvious.

## Design directions

### Direction A — Berlin After Dark (recommended foundation)

The current cinematic direction becomes more specifically Berlin: deep burgundy and black, sodium-gold light, raw concrete textures, flash photography, oversized condensed type and moving particles.

Signature interactions:

- Opening video behaves like entering the venue.
- Each current event is a “room” with its own color accent and motion behavior.
- Artist previews arrive as overlapping posters rather than a generic card grid.
- Schedule changes from workshop-light to party-dark as the day progresses.
- Ticket action remains visible without covering content.

Benefits:

- Immediately recognizable in an Instagram click-through.
- Feels premium without becoming corporate.
- Works with the real party footage and existing posters.
- Can evolve each year without changing the menu structure.

Risk: continuous video and effects must remain optional for reduced-motion users and carefully optimized for mobile.

### Direction B — The Living Archive

The website feels like an editorial contact sheet and physical event archive. Film strips, handwritten credits, date stamps, photographer notes and year selectors turn the large historical library into an advantage.

Signature interactions:

- Scrub through years on a horizontal timeline.
- Open an event “box” containing its poster, recap, albums, credits and next related edition.
- Random “from the archive” moments can appear between current-event sections.

Benefits:

- No other comparable bachata festival can easily imitate years of genuine history.
- Gives the NAS gallery a clear public purpose.
- Encourages repeat visits after an event and provides strong social sharing material.

Risk: the archive should enrich current ticket sales, not dominate the first screen.

### Direction C — Explosion Passport

Visitors choose the kind of dancer/weekend they want, then receive a visual route through the relevant event.

Possible routes:

- “I want the full international festival” → BBF.
- “I want to learn both roles” → Role Rotation.
- “I want a focused small-room experience” → Elite Dance.
- “I want to compete” → Jack & Jill.
- “I was there” → photo archive.

Benefits:

- Solves the challenge of one brand containing several different formats.
- Makes discovery playful while preserving the existing pages.
- Creates clear intent data that can later improve event promotion.

Risk: it should be an optional discovery layer, never the only navigation.

### Direction D — Day / Night

The page gradually moves from warm workshop daylight into saturated party night. Day sections explain learning, artists and venue; night sections sell socials, shows, music and community.

Benefits:

- Communicates the complete weekend emotionally.
- Creates natural scroll progression.
- Provides a distinctive visual logic for schedules and artist types.

Risk: less suitable as the only identity for Role Rotation or historical albums, so it works best inside individual event pages.

### Direction E — Explosion Control Room

The site becomes a live festival console: “Now / Next / Prepare / Relive.” Countdown, ticket phase, latest artist drop, venue readiness, schedule and travel information behave like signals on a dashboard.

Benefits:

- Extremely useful in the final weeks before an event.
- Makes rapidly changing information feel intentional.
- Can later power an event-day mode optimized for phones.

Risk: too much interface styling could feel like a technology product. Use it as a functional layer, not the entire visual identity.

## Recommended combined direction

Use **Berlin After Dark** as the permanent visual identity, **Explosion Passport** for choosing among current events, **The Living Archive** for historical material, and a light **Control Room** layer during active festival periods.

This combination keeps the existing structure while giving each content type the presentation it deserves:

```text
Home
  → Next event / tickets
  → Choose your Explosion
  → Current highlights
  → Community and team
  → From the living archive

Event pages
  → Campaign hero
  → Artists / program / venue / tickets / FAQ
  → Related competition or albums

Archive
  → Year → event → album → photograph

Contact
  → Choose topic → short routed form → direct email fallback
```

## Contact and email direction

### Public experience

The contact page should behave like a signal router rather than a generic message box. Visitors first choose a topic—tickets, group discounts, partnerships, media, volunteering or general—and then see one short form with that topic preselected.

### Initial delivery

All form notifications should initially go to the existing `info@bachataexplosion.com` inbox through one protected form endpoint. The topic remains in every submission, allowing one mailbox to filter messages without paying for more accounts.

Recommended future aliases, all forwarding to the same inbox until volume justifies separation:

- `tickets@bachataexplosion.com`
- `partners@bachataexplosion.com`
- `privacy@bachataexplosion.com`

Do not publish these aliases until they have been created and tested.

### Provider boundary

- Website form: Formspree or an equivalent protected form endpoint.
- Newsletter: MailerLite or another dedicated newsletter provider with double opt-in.
- Business email: keep the IONOS mailbox active during the website transition; migrate it separately only after a complete IMAP backup and DNS plan.

The website endpoint remains an environment setting, so changing form providers does not require redesigning the page.
